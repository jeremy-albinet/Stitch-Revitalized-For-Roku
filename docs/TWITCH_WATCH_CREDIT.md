# Twitch watch credit and chat events

Research started October 23, 2026; experimental implementation added October 24. Subscription/gift/raid notices, Bits/reward message context, a Hype Train banner and pinned messages are implemented. A physical Roku displayed real trains, a subscription notice and pins. A follow-up native viewing comparison advanced Pokémon reward progress, with stable counters during idle controls. Basic pause, hidden-chat, quality-change and exit checks passed. Streaks and recap credit remain unverified. The three network features are separately opt-in and off by default.

## What Stitch currently sends

Playback resolves a Twitch playback token through GraphQL and loads HLS through Usher, directly or through Stitch's playback workers. The new `TwitchEngagementTask` can report actual live playback when **Twitch watch credit** is enabled and the viewer is signed in. The optional `AnalyticsTask` remains a separate service; enabling app diagnostics does not enable watch reporting.

Chat requests `twitch.tv/tags` and `twitch.tv/commands`. Before this change, `ChatJob` queued only `PRIVMSG`, silently discarding `USERNOTICE`. Authenticated chat is attempted only over secure WebSocket transport on supported devices. Older devices retain anonymous read-only chat. Neither chat membership nor sending chat messages establishes account watch credit.

## Watch credit: separate outcomes

- **Drops, promotional items and some badges:** Twitch tracks eligible viewing and decides when a reward is earned. Its [Drops viewer guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/mission-based-drops?language=en_US&sfdcIFrameOrigin=null) currently excludes TV and console apps. The [developer Drops API](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/drops/technical-guide/) serves game organizations fulfilling earned rewards; it is not an API for a viewing client to add minutes or award itself entitlements.
- **Watch streaks and Channel Points:** Twitch's [Watch Streaks guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/recover-watch-streaks) requires a signed-in account and a channel with Channel Points. It warns that TV apps may count unreliably, and even chatting does not guarantee streak recognition. A milestone announcement in chat only displays an event Twitch already generated.
- **Annual recap:** no documented client API to submit recap minutes or read an immediately updated per-viewer recap counter was found. Success for Channel Points or Drops would not independently prove recap inclusion.
- **Streamer bot loyalty rewards:** these may use chat presence and their own rules, rather than Twitch's viewing ledger. Supporting Twitch watch telemetry would not guarantee credit from every bot.

Anonymous playback must remain available. Account-specific credit cannot be meaningfully verified without the viewer's Twitch identity.

## Experimental watch reporting

Xtra, a third-party Android client, implements a `minute-watched` event in [PlayerRepository](https://github.com/crackededed/Xtra/blob/96772030c7712581ad150c9b8b619c77a3527e50/app/src/main/java/com/github/andreyasadchy/xtra/repository/PlayerRepository.kt). It discovers a telemetry endpoint from Twitch's channel page and versioned settings script, then posts an encoded event identifying the viewer, channel and broadcast. Its [chat-side callback](https://github.com/crackededed/Xtra/blob/96772030c7712581ad150c9b8b619c77a3527e50/app/src/main/java/com/github/andreyasadchy/xtra/ui/chat/ChatViewModel.kt) invokes that method. These are implementation references, not Twitch documentation or evidence of an awarded reward.

A read-only check on the research date found the same discovery path in Twitch's public page:

- Channel page: `https://www.twitch.tv/twitch`.
- Advertised script: `https://assets.twitch.tv/config/settings.a346c0e26e35ab87386587f3d6bd61f2.js`.
- Advertised telemetry destination: `https://beacon.twitch.tv/track`.

That discovery check fetched only the public page and script and sent no watch events. The later native account test is recorded below; no fabricated viewing was reported. The versioned script will rotate. Endpoint discovery and HTTP acceptance alone do not demonstrate watch credit.

The implemented prototype has these boundaries:

- One worker is owned by the selected LIVE channel in `VideoPlayer`. All three settings disabled means no worker. Anonymous watch-only playback and recorded playback also create no worker. Watch reporting continues with chat hidden; public Hype Train and pinned-message polling do not require sign-in.
- OAuth validation obtains the actual account ID and checks the existing client ID, account login and token lifetime. The test TV returned HTTP 200 with `expires_in: 0`; this receives a bounded one-hour validation lease rather than being rejected or trusted indefinitely. Identities are revalidated after 55 minutes, or sooner for shorter leases. Transport failures retry after 30 seconds with exponential backoff capped at five minutes; they preserve the existing identity without extending its lease. An expired lease suspends reporting while public chat polling continues. HTTP 401/403 and identity mismatches end credentialed reporting for that worker. Device identity is not an account ID. Logout or a changed token stops the old owner. No login scopes are changed. Twitch requires validation at startup and at least hourly in its [validation guide](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/authentication/validate-tokens/).
- The scene publishes fresh position/state samples with a monotonic clock, sequence and wrapper generation. Only forward playback accumulates time. Pauses, buffering, stalls, seeks, stale gaps and wrapper replacements add no time. A quality switch preserves legitimately accumulated partial time while resetting its sample baseline. The Task also collects fresh samples during asynchronous HTTP waits, preserving the same five-second admission bound rather than treating network latency as a playback stall.
- The worker resolves the current channel and broadcast IDs through a small GraphQL query. Broadcast changes reset accumulated time. It refreshes that identity before submitting a completed minute. Earned minutes remain pending through failed pre-submit queries and pauses, with a one-minute retry delay. A confirmed broadcast change/offline state discards old credit. A minute is consumed only when its POST is attempted; ambiguous POSTs are never retried, and backlog is sent in order at no more than one attempt per minute.
- Telemetry is form-encoded and identifies the actual user, channel, broadcast and playback session. The observed compatibility discriminator is `player: "site"`; `backend: "roku_scenegraph"` identifies the actual backend. This undocumented contract is a candidate for provider testing, not evidence that Roku viewing is credited.
- OAuth credentials go only to Twitch's fixed validation endpoint. Discovery accepts the versioned `assets.twitch.tv/config/settings.*.js` path and the exact advertised `https://beacon.twitch.tv/track` destination. Telemetry contains neither the token nor the device code. Requests have finite deadlines and response admission limits; responses are size-checked after receipt rather than establishing a native heap bound.
- Safe console summaries and `VideoPlayer.engagementStatus` expose `completedMinutes`, `attemptedMinutes`, `acceptedMinutes`, `lastHttpStatus`, `watchState`, `hypeState` and `pinState`. **Accepted means HTTP transport acceptance, not a reward, streak or recap award.** No identities, tokens or source URLs are logged.

The native session below records Pokémon progress during signed-in Roku playback, with idle controls and the stated assumption that other playing sessions were closed following the user's reply. No automatic claiming, chat posting, reward farming or extra viewing session is implemented.

## Chat notices implemented

`USERNOTICE` now uses the existing ordered, delayed, bounded message queue. The renderer displays Twitch's system text in the existing link accent color, followed by the optional user's name, badges and message. System text is kept as text, even if a word matches a cached emote. Twitch emote offsets continue to refer to the original user message. Escaped line breaks in the system text become spaces.

This supports server-supplied subscription/resubscription notices, individual and community gifts, anonymous gifts, raids, Bits badge milestones, watch-streak shares, announcements and other notices containing displayable text. It does not synthesize missing notices. See Twitch's [IRC reference](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/irc/#usernotice-tags).

Both system-only notices and notices with user text retain their message and actor identities, so deletions and user purges apply to the complete rendered event. Empty/malformed notices and oversized text are rejected. Existing queue limits, delivery handshake and cleanup remain in use; busy channels can still exceed the bounded queue. The IRC notice path adds no Task, timer, OAuth scope or credential path.

Notices are received only while live chat is connected. This adds neither event history nor recorded chat replay. Bits-tagged messages now show their Bits amount, and custom-reward-tagged messages show Channel Points context, while preserving the original text and emotes. Animated Cheermotes and a financial activity ledger are outside this implementation.

## Hype Trains

The documented [Hype Train EventSub subscriptions](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/#channelhype_trainbegin) require `channel:read:hype_train`, granted by the relevant broadcaster. The [Get Hype Train Status endpoint](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/reference/#get-hype-train-status) also requires that scope and a token belonging to the requested broadcaster. A viewer authorizing Stitch cannot grant that scope on someone else's behalf. Adding it to the viewer login screen would not provide access to every channel's train.

The current private viewer interface provides another path. Twitch's own [Hype Train context bundle](https://assets.twitch.tv/assets/46694-860044133cd59ea30d58.js) uses `user.channel.hypeTrain.execution`; its query is named `GetHypeTrainExecution`. The underlying selection was read from the current app and tested with Stitch's existing client ID and an anonymous browser identity. A real response for xQc contained Level 3, progress 1000, goal 5300 and an expiry with nanosecond precision. No broadcaster token was used. This demonstrates viewer data access, not native Roku acceptance.

The new worker polls the small viewer query every 30 seconds while inactive and every 10 seconds while a train is present. **Hype Trains in chat** enables this only while chat is visible; a watch-only worker omits the Hype Train selection. Polling works without a native WebSocket and does not use the old public PubSub service.

The chat banner shows the provider's level, current percentage and remaining time. It uses the provider expiry and the last successful snapshot's fetch time, hides stale data after 45 seconds, briefly shows an explicit recent end, and releases its timer on disposal. Re-publishing a status counter cannot make old train data fresh. It does not extrapolate progress from gifts, invent train events or reconstruct a financial ledger. Polling may miss short transitions, and live level/progress/end transitions still need device verification.

Do not start new work against the old public PubSub endpoint: Twitch [decommissioned it on April 14, 2025](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/pubsub/). Internal viewer transports seen in other clients are separate, undocumented interfaces.

## Pinned messages

Twitch's current [Get Pinned Chat Message endpoint](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/reference/#get-pinned-chat-message) requires moderator authorization. The viewer interface used by Twitch's current web chat exposes `channel.pinnedChatMessages(first: 1, messageType: MOD)` without that privilege. Browser requests with Stitch's existing client ID and an anonymous device identity returned both timed and indefinite moderator pins. This is an undocumented viewer interface and can change independently of the documented API. No new login scope or moderator action is implemented.

**Pinned messages in chat** enables a separate public query every 15 seconds while live chat is visible. Anonymous viewing works; the pins-only worker does not read an OAuth token or send watch telemetry. A pin-query failure hides the pin without invalidating watch reporting. An unrelated broadcast/Hype query failure preserves the last pin and its original freshness deadline. Unknown broadcast state defers new pin queries; a confirmed broadcast change/offline state clears the previous pin. Public broadcast metadata must indicate a live stream: Twitch can return indefinite pins on offline channels, so their presence alone is insufficient.

The panel above chat shows the original author's name and a bounded plain-text preview. With player controls hidden, **Down** opens the full text; **Back** or **Continue watching** closes it while playback continues. Roku's Video node can intercept the star button for its system overlay, so the app uses Down. The text preserves emote codes and links as text; it does not render pin emotes, open links or support paid/power-up pins. Hype Trains and pins have separate stacked headers.

Pins expire using the provider deadline and hide after 45 seconds without a successful fresh query. Explicitly indefinite pins remain visible only with fresh polling and a live broadcast. Unpinning, expiry, chat hide, channel change and disposal close the full-text dialog and release or stop its timer as appropriate. IRC message deletion, user purge and room clear suppress matching old pin snapshots; a deleted current message stays suppressed after the bounded moderation window rolls. Polling can delay updates or miss brief changes; a channel transition resets moderation state.

Local checks cover normalization, explicit indefinite versus missing expiry, malformed timestamps/content, valid empty connections, public pins-only operation and pin failures during legitimate reporting. The actual Chat fixture passes 68 assertions including full-text dialog ownership, both headers, stale snapshots, expiry, deletion/purge/room clear, moderation-window rollover and teardown. The Player fixture passes 29 assertions including a real engine Down-key event, anonymous pins-only workers and stale-owner refusal.

The October 24 G204X session displayed Loeya's real long timed pin in the preview. The final Down-key candidate also displayed a real pin by Jimmy_TheBeard on alison_ace and opened its complete text in a native dialog. Back closed it without leaving playback; reopening and selecting Continue watching also closed it, and native media kept advancing. Channel replacement cleared the previous pin. After Loeya's provider deadline of 16:21:58 UTC, reopening that channel showed no pin; a separate public query also returned an empty connection. This is an expired-state check, not an observation of the in-place transition. The first attempt used the star button; the Video/system overlay intercepted it before the parent handler, so that binding was replaced and rechecked. No pin/unpin or chat-posting request was sent. Native expiry/unpin transitions, longer full-text scrolling, concurrent Hype/pin layout and older-device readability remain acceptance checks.

## Test TV acceptance

1. Install the normal package, sign in with the intended test account, and enable **Twitch watch credit**, **Hype Trains in chat** and **Pinned messages in chat** in Settings as needed. Reopen a live stream. All three settings default to off; account preferences are separate from anonymous preferences.
2. For a time-based reward, link the relevant game account and record the current Twitch Drops inventory progress. Confirm that the account can earn progress in the official player, then close every other playing Twitch window/app during the Stitch comparison. Viewing the inventory alone is sufficient to inspect progress.
3. Watch one eligible live stream continuously for several minutes. Allow initial identity/discovery work before the first complete minute. Capture `[TwitchEngagement]` status lines and compare Twitch's actual reward progress. An HTTP success without an inventory increase is not a successful credit test.
4. Pause for more than a minute, then resume. After any already in-flight request finishes, no further attempts should appear while paused. Test a stall, a quality change, hidden chat and chat reopen. A quality change must not create a burst or a second reporter.
5. Observe an actual active Hype Train, a level/progress update and its end. Confirm banner placement/readability, expiry and hide/show behavior. With no active train, the banner should be absent. Observe a real subscription/gift/raid notice, a resub with emotes and a Bits message.
6. Switch channels, leave with Back, sign out and reopen. Verify no late banner from the old channel, no continued old-account reports and no reporter on VODs/clips. Anonymous viewing must continue; anonymous Hype Train viewing can be enabled independently.
7. Record Channel Points/streak changes separately from Drops. Recap inclusion needs its own later evidence. Keep watch reporting experimental while completing isolated provider and lifecycle acceptance; the native results above do not establish all reward types or all devices.
8. Observe a real moderator pin. With controls hidden, press Down, read the full text, close with Back/Continue watching and confirm playback continues. Check a long pin, replacement/unpin, expiry, concurrent Hype Train, hide/show and switching to a channel without a pin. The previous pin and dialog must disappear.
